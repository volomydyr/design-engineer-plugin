# StoryBrand Canvas Template

## Overview

The StoryBrand framework follows a seven-part narrative structure where the user is the hero, and your product is the guide. This canvas captures the complete messaging framework that can be applied to landing pages, marketing, email campaigns, product branding, website copy, and UX microcopy.

The core principle: top brands do not talk about themselves. They talk about the user -- their problems, their struggles, and their transformation. The product is the path, not the protagonist.

---

## 1. The Hero (Your User)

**Who is the main character?**

Write a 2-3 sentence description of the hero. This should be drawn directly from your user persona.

**What do they want?**

State the hero's desire in one clear sentence. This must be something specific and tangible that the user can imagine achieving.

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Who they are | [Role, context, situation] |
| What they want | [Specific, tangible desire] |
| Why they want it | [Underlying motivation] |

**Example:**
> A mid-level designer who wants to systematically grow their skills so they can confidently apply for senior roles -- not just hope they are "ready someday."

---

## 2. The Problem

Every compelling story has a problem with three layers. All three must be present for the messaging to resonate emotionally.

### 2.1 External Problem

The tangible, visible obstacle. This is what the hero would describe if you asked them "what is wrong?"

> [One sentence describing the external problem]

**Example:**
> Learning resources are scattered across dozens of browser tabs, Telegram chats, and bookmarks with no way to organize or prioritize them.

### 2.2 Internal Problem

The emotional struggle that accompanies the external problem. This is how the problem makes the hero feel.

> [One sentence describing the internal feeling]

**Example:**
> They feel overwhelmed and guilty -- they know they should be learning, but the chaos makes it feel impossible to even start.

### 2.3 Philosophical Problem

The deeper principle at stake. Why is it just plain wrong that this problem exists? This connects to a universal value.

> [One sentence stating why this is wrong on a fundamental level]

**Example:**
> Talented professionals should not be held back in their careers just because no one taught them how to learn effectively.

---

## 3. The Guide (Your Product)

The guide must demonstrate two qualities:

### 3.1 Empathy

Show that you understand the hero's pain. Write 1-2 sentences that prove you "get it."

> [Empathy statement]

**Example:**
> We know what it is like to have a hundred open tabs of "things to learn later" and the sinking feeling that "later" never comes.

### 3.2 Authority

Show that you can actually help. This is not bragging -- it is proof. Use one of these:

- Testimonials or user numbers (when available)
- Methodology or framework (if your approach is unique)
- Results or outcomes (specific, measurable)

> [Authority statement]

**Example:**
> Built by a designer who spent a year researching exactly how tech professionals learn best, testing with over 100 real users.

---

## 4. The Plan

Give the hero a simple path to follow. The plan should have 3-4 steps maximum. Each step must be concrete and easy to understand.

| Step | Action | What Happens |
|------|--------|-------------|
| 1 | [Simple action verb + what to do] | [What the user gets or experiences] |
| 2 | [Simple action verb + what to do] | [What the user gets or experiences] |
| 3 | [Simple action verb + what to do] | [What the user gets or experiences] |

**Example:**

| Step | Action | What Happens |
|------|--------|-------------|
| 1 | Import your learning resources | All your scattered links organized in one place |
| 2 | Get your personalized skill map | See exactly where you stand and what to learn next |
| 3 | Follow bite-sized learning plans | Build skills that matter for your career, week by week |

---

## 5. Call to Action

### 5.1 Primary CTA (Direct)

The one thing you want the hero to do right now. This should be a single, clear, action-oriented phrase.

> [Primary CTA text]

**Example:**
> "Start your free skill assessment"

### 5.2 Secondary CTA (Transitional)

A softer option for people who are not ready to commit. This builds trust and keeps the relationship open.

> [Secondary CTA text]

**Example:**
> "Join the waitlist for early access"

---

## 6. Success (The Happy Ending)

Paint a vivid picture of what the hero's life looks like after your product helps them. Address three dimensions:

### 6.1 Status Change

> How does the hero's position or identity change?

**Example:**
> They go from "someone who means to learn" to "someone who actively grows every week."

### 6.2 Emotional Transformation

> How do they feel differently?

**Example:**
> Confident, in control of their career path, and no longer anxious about falling behind.

### 6.3 Daily Life Impact

> What is concretely different in their day-to-day?

**Example:**
> Instead of 40 open tabs and guilt, they spend 20 focused minutes per day on exactly the right material.

---

## 7. Failure (The Consequences of Inaction)

What happens if the hero does nothing? This creates urgency without being manipulative. Be honest about real consequences.

> [2-3 sentences describing what the hero risks losing or missing]

**Example:**
> Without a system, learning resources keep piling up. Career growth stalls as peers with structured development plans move ahead. The gap between "where I am" and "where I want to be" gets wider every month.

---

## 8. The One-Liner

Condense the entire StoryBrand into a single sentence that can be used as a tagline, elevator pitch, or social media bio.

**Formula:**
> [Problem] + [Solution] + [Result]

**Example:**
> Stop drowning in learning resources and start growing the skills that actually advance your career.

---

## 9. Application Guide

Use the completed canvas to create messaging for:

| Application | Which Elements to Emphasize |
|------------|---------------------------|
| Landing page headline | The Hero's desire + The One-Liner |
| Landing page subheadline | External Problem + Empathy statement |
| Landing page body | The Plan (step by step) + Success vision |
| Landing page CTA | Primary and Secondary CTAs |
| Email subject lines | Internal Problem + Failure consequences |
| Social media posts | One-liner + Success stories |
| Onboarding screens | The Plan steps + Empathy |
| UX microcopy | Empathy + specific step context |
| Ad copy | External Problem + Primary CTA |

---

## 10. Messaging Test

Before finalizing, run the messaging through these checks:

- [ ] **The grunt test**: If someone saw your landing page for 5 seconds, could they answer: What do you offer? How will it make my life better? What do I do next?
- [ ] **Hero check**: Is the user the hero in every sentence, or has the brand crept back into the spotlight?
- [ ] **Specificity check**: Are the outcomes concrete and measurable, or vague and generic?
- [ ] **Emotional check**: Does the messaging address feelings, not just features?
- [ ] **Simplicity check**: Could a 12-year-old understand the plan?
