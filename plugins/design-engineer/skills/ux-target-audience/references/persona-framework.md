# Persona Framework

A user persona helps you stay focused on who you're building the product for. Every time a new idea pops into your head, check it against the persona. This way, you avoid one of the most common traps — building features instead of solving real needs.

Start with a **proto-persona**: your best guess of who the user is, even without solid data to back it up. Later, refine it as you gather insights from surveys and user tests. There is no universal template — the details always depend on your product.

---

## Core Structure

Focus on four categories:

### 1. Background

Their role, environment, and daily situation.

| Field | Content |
|-------|---------|
| Name | A realistic name (not "User A") |
| Role | Job title or primary occupation |
| Experience | Years of experience, career stage |
| Context | Employment type, company size, industry |
| One-line summary | A single sentence that captures who this person is |

**Example:**
> Olena, 28, mid-level product designer at a mid-size tech company. Three years in design, recently started exploring AI tools but unsure where to start.

### 2. Behaviors

Describe observable actions, not intentions. How they act, make decisions, or approach the problem area.

- **Current problem-solving approach**: What they actually do today when they encounter the problem
- **Tool usage**: Specific products, apps, or workarounds they rely on
- **Decision patterns**: How they choose between options (research-driven, peer recommendation, trial and error)

**Anti-pattern:** "They want to improve" is not a behavior. "They save learning links in browser tabs and Telegram channels but rarely return to them" is a behavior.

### 3. Pain Points

What frustrates them most about today's solutions. List 3-5 specific pain points, ordered from most to least severe.

### 4. Goals

What "better" looks like for them. List 3-5 goals the user wants to achieve — both short-term (next 1-3 months) and aspirational (next 6-12 months).

## Understanding Motivation with BMap Questions

Use these three questions from the BMap framework during user research to understand what drives and blocks the persona's behavior. Adapt the bracketed parts to your specific product and problem.

**Q1 — Hope (The Magic Wand Question):**
> "If you had a magic wand and could instantly [achieve the value your product promises], how would that change your life?"

Reveals the user's underlying motivation — what they truly want to achieve.

**Q2 — Pain (The Biggest Challenge Question):**
> "What is your #1 challenge when it comes to [the problem your product solves]? And why is it so challenging?"

Uncovers friction in their current experience.

**Q3 — Barrier (The Last Attempt Question):**
> "Tell me about the last time you did [the action your product enables], what was preventing you from [reaching the goal]?"

Identifies what stands between the user and success.

These questions feed back into the persona — use the answers to refine the Behaviors, Pain Points, and Goals sections above.
