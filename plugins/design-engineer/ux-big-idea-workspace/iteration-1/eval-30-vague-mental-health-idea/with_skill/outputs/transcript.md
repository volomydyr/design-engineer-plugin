## Step 1: Determine Starting Point

Classified user as "Vague idea, needs shaping." The skill's starting-point assessment identified this as the earliest stage -- no concrete concept yet, just a domain interest.

---

## Step 2: Probing Questions to Narrow Focus

Used the skill's structured question framework to explore the mental health space:

1. **What drew you to this space?** Personal experience seeing friends struggle. Counseling center waitlists of 3-6 weeks.
2. **What specific aspect of mental health?** Anxiety and isolation, especially for first-year students transitioning.
3. **What exists today?** BetterHelp (expensive for students), campus counseling (long waits), Calm/Headspace (generic, not student-specific).
4. **What gap do you see?** Peer support that's accessible immediately without clinical overhead.

---

## Step 3: Concept Crystallization

From the probing questions, shaped the vague idea into a concrete concept: "An anonymous peer support platform for college students that matches them with trained student listeners for real-time chat during anxiety episodes."

---

## Step 4: Viability Assessment

Applied viability criteria:
- **Problem severity**: High. Student mental health crisis is well-documented.
- **Market size**: 20M+ US college students, ~40% report anxiety.
- **Competition**: Crisis Text Line (crisis only), 7 Cups (not student-specific), campus services (slow).
- **Differentiation**: Peer-to-peer, campus-specific, anonymous, immediate.

---

## Step 5: Risk Identification

1. **Liability risk**: What happens if a user is in genuine crisis? Need escalation protocols.
2. **Regulatory concerns**: Does this constitute mental health services? HIPAA implications?
3. **Listener burnout**: Trained student volunteers may experience compassion fatigue.
4. **Trust and safety**: Anonymous platform risks misuse.
