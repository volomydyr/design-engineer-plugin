# Meeting Preparation Template

Fill out this template before walking into any product review. If there are answers you are unsure of, you should go back to the drawing table and figure out a few things so you can better back your decisions.

---

## Pre-Meeting Questions

### 1. What is the goal of the review?

_Define the specific outcome you want from this meeting. "Get approval for X" is better than "Show the team the new designs."_

**Your answer**: [What specific decision or feedback do you need?]

### 2. What do I want feedback on?

_Create guardrails. Do not show designs without specifically asking what you want feedback on. This focuses the conversation and makes team decisions quickly._

**Your answer**: [List 1-2 specific areas where you need input]

**What is NOT open for discussion**: [List decisions already made]

### 3. What problem does this solution solve?

_Frame the problem clearly. Make sure it is a real user problem, not just a business wish._

**Your answer**: [The specific user problem, backed by data or research]

**Is it a business problem?** [Yes/No – if yes, also frame the user impact]

### 4. How does it currently affect our users?

_Tell the story. What is the user experiencing right now? Use real quotes, real data, real scenarios._

**The story**: [User-centered narrative showing the current pain point]

**Supporting data**: [Metrics, user quotes, research findings]

### 5. Why is this solution better than the alternatives?

_Back your decisions with psychological principles. Name the specific principles and explain why they apply._

**Your answer**: [Solution rationale with named principles]

**Alternatives considered**: [What else you explored and why it was less effective]

## During the Meeting

### Reminders

- Lead with the user story, not the solution
- Use psychological vocabulary to describe decisions
- If feedback goes off-track, redirect: "That is a great point. For today, I need input specifically on [X]. Can we capture that for a follow-up?"
- When receiving feedback, follow the 3-step process: Lead with yes, Repeat and Empathize, Assure

### Key sentences to keep handy

1. "How can we align the copy with the benefits..."
2. "It facilitates the users' goal..."
3. "How can we overcome this user's barrier..."
4. "Users will block this because..."
5. "It draws the user's attention..."
6. "The framing is not exactly aligned with..."
7. "It emphasizes users' benefits..."
8. "How can we reduce friction even more..."
9. "Could we nudge people to..."
