# StoryBrand Canvas Template

StoryBrand is a storytelling method where the user is the main hero, and your product is simply the guide that helps them succeed. It is used far beyond tech — authors and screenwriters apply the same structure.

It helps you communicate ideas in a way that connects emotionally, and sell an idea when you don't have a product yet. Use it for marketing, social posts, email, ad campaigns, product branding, website copy, UX microcopy — basically anywhere words and your business intersect.

The core principle: most businesses talk about themselves — how great their product is and how advanced their features are. But people only care about themselves. Top brands don't talk about "me." They talk about you: your problems, your struggles, your transformation.

## The 7-Part Canvas

### 1. The Hero (Your User)

Who is the main character? What do they want? Draw directly from your user persona.

State the hero's desire in one clear sentence — something specific and tangible they can imagine achieving.

**Example:**
> A mid-level designer who wants to systematically grow their skills so they can confidently apply for senior roles.

### 2. The Problem

Every compelling story has a problem with three layers:

**External Problem** — The tangible, visible obstacle.
> Learning resources are scattered across dozens of browser tabs, Telegram chats, and bookmarks with no way to organize them.

**Internal Problem** — The emotional struggle that accompanies it.
> They feel overwhelmed and guilty — they know they should be learning, but the chaos makes it feel impossible to start.

**Philosophical Problem** — Why is it just plain wrong that this problem exists?
> Talented professionals should not be held back in their careers just because no one taught them how to learn effectively.

### 3. The Guide (Your Product)

The guide demonstrates two qualities:

**Empathy** — Show you understand the hero's pain.
> We know what it is like to have a hundred open tabs of "things to learn later" and the sinking feeling that "later" never comes.

**Authority** — Show you can actually help. Use testimonials, methodology, or measurable results.
> Built by a designer who spent a year researching how tech professionals learn best, testing with over 100 real users.

### 4. The Plan

Give the hero a simple path. 3-4 steps maximum, each concrete and easy to understand.

| Step | Action | What Happens |
|------|--------|-------------|
| 1 | Import your learning resources | All your scattered links organized in one place |
| 2 | Get your personalized skill map | See exactly where you stand and what to learn next |
| 3 | Follow bite-sized learning plans | Build skills that matter for your career, week by week |

### 5. Call to Action

**Primary CTA (Direct):** The one thing you want them to do right now.
> "Start your free skill assessment"

**Secondary CTA (Transitional):** A softer option for people not ready to commit.
> "Join the waitlist for early access"

### 6. Success (The Happy Ending)

Paint a vivid picture of the hero's life after your product helps them.

> They go from "someone who means to learn" to "someone who actively grows every week." Confident, in control of their career path, no longer anxious about falling behind. Instead of 40 open tabs and guilt, they spend 20 focused minutes per day on exactly the right material.

### 7. Failure (The Consequences of Inaction)

What happens if the hero does nothing? Be honest about real consequences.

> Without a system, learning resources keep piling up. Career growth stalls as peers with structured development plans move ahead. The gap between "where I am" and "where I want to be" gets wider every month.

## Complete Canvas at a Glance

Here's what all 7 elements look like together as one cohesive narrative (for a learning tool aimed at designers):

**Hero:** A mid-level designer who wants to systematically grow their skills so they can confidently apply for senior roles.

**Problem:**
- External: Learning resources are scattered across browser tabs, Telegram chats, and bookmarks with no way to organize them

**Plan:**

Notice how each element flows into the next: the hero's desire sets up the problem, the problem creates the need for a guide, the guide provides a plan, and the plan leads to either success or failure. This narrative arc is what makes StoryBrand effective.
