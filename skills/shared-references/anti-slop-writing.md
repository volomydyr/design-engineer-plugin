# Anti-slop writing reference

Read this file before generating any text for deliverables, prototypes, UI copy, or landing pages. Apply the rules below to everything you write.

Based on the humanizer skill (v2.5.1) by blader, which is based on Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" guide maintained by WikiProject AI Cleanup.

---

## Two modes

### Product copy (prototypes, landing pages, UI text, buttons, headings)
- Concise, benefit-driven, human
- Active voice, present tense
- Specific numbers, not vague claims
- Short, scannable sentences
- Match the tone from the user's StoryBrand or problem statement – real language, not marketing fluff

### Deliverable copy (documents, analyses, reports, assessments)
- Plain, direct, no consultant-speak
- Sound like a smart colleague writing notes, not a consultant writing a report
- "Users hate copy-pasting feedback" not "Users experience significant friction in the feedback communication workflow"

## Content patterns to avoid

### 1. Inflated significance and legacy language
**Words to watch:** stands/serves as, is a testament/reminder, a vital/significant/crucial/pivotal/key role/moment, underscores/highlights its importance, reflects broader, symbolizing its ongoing/enduring/lasting, setting the stage for, represents/marks a shift, key turning point, evolving landscape, focal point, indelible mark

**Fix:** State what happened, not why it matters cosmically.

### 2. Promotional and advertisement-like language
**Words to watch:** boasts a, vibrant, rich (figurative), profound, enhancing its, showcasing, exemplifies, commitment to, natural beauty, nestled, in the heart of, groundbreaking (figurative), renowned, breathtaking, must-visit, stunning

**Fix:** Neutral tone. Specific details over adjective piles.

### 3. Vague attributions
**Words to watch:** Industry reports, Observers have cited, Experts argue, Some critics argue, Research suggests (without citation)

**Fix:** Name the source or drop the claim. Never invent statistics.

### 4. Formulaic "challenges and future" sections
**Words to watch:** Despite its... faces several challenges..., Despite these challenges, Future Outlook

**Fix:** Name a specific challenge with a date and context.

## Language and grammar patterns to avoid

### 5. Overused AI vocabulary
**High-frequency AI words:** Additionally, align with, crucial, delve, emphasizing, enduring, enhance, fostering, garner, highlight (verb), interplay, intricate/intricacies, key (adjective), landscape (abstract noun), pivotal, showcase, tapestry (abstract noun), testament, underscore (verb), valuable, vibrant, leverage, streamline, seamless, comprehensive, robust

**Fix:** Use simpler, more direct words.

### 6. Copula avoidance ("serves as" instead of "is")
**Words to watch:** serves as/stands as/marks/represents [a], boasts/features/offers [a]

**Fix:** Just use "is" or "are" or "has."

### 7. Negative parallelisms ("this is not X – this is Y")
**Forbidden pattern:** "It's not just about X; it's about Y" / "The pain isn't X – it's Y" / "This is not X. It's Y."

**Fix:** State what it IS, directly.

### 8. Rule of three overuse
**Fix:** Don't force ideas into groups of three. Two is fine. Four is fine. Say what needs saying.

### 9. Synonym cycling
**Fix:** Don't alternate between "protagonist," "main character," "central figure," and "hero" in successive sentences. Pick one and use it.

### 10. Passive voice and subjectless fragments
**Fix:** Name the actor. "The system preserves results" not "Results are preserved."

## Style patterns to avoid

### 11. Em dash overuse
**Fix:** Replace em dashes with commas, periods, or parentheses. The plugin's rule is en dashes only (`–`), but even en dashes should be used sparingly.

### 12. Excessive boldface
**Fix:** Bold for actual emphasis, not for every noun phrase.

### 13. Inline-header vertical lists ("**Speed:** faster performance")
**Fix:** Write as prose when possible. Lists for genuinely separate items.

### 14. Title case in headings
**Fix:** Sentence case only. "Strategic negotiations" not "Strategic Negotiations."

### 15. Emojis
**Fix:** No emojis unless the user explicitly requests them.

## Communication patterns to avoid

### 16. Collaborative artifacts
**Words to watch:** I hope this helps, Of course!, Certainly!, You're absolutely right!, Would you like..., let me know, here is a...

**Fix:** Start with the content, not the pleasantries.

### 17. Sycophantic tone
**Fix:** Don't praise the user's question before answering it.

### 18. Filler phrases
- "In order to" → "To"
- "Due to the fact that" → "Because"
- "At this point in time" → "Now"
- "It is important to note that" → (delete, just state it)
- "Has the ability to" → "Can"

### 19. Excessive hedging
**Fix:** "The policy may affect outcomes" not "It could potentially possibly be argued that the policy might have some effect."

### 20. Generic positive conclusions
**Fix:** End with a specific next step, not "The future looks bright."

### 21. Signposting and announcements
**Words to watch:** Let's dive in, let's explore, let's break this down, here's what you need to know, without further ado

**Fix:** Just start. Don't announce what you're about to do.

### 22. Persuasive authority tropes
**Words to watch:** The real question is, at its core, in reality, what really matters, fundamentally, the heart of the matter

**Fix:** State the point without ceremonial framing.

## Personality and soul

Avoiding AI patterns is only half the job. Sterile, voiceless writing is just as obvious as slop.

### Signs of soulless writing (even if technically "clean"):
- Every sentence is the same length and structure
- No opinions, just neutral reporting
- No acknowledgment of uncertainty or mixed feelings
- No first-person perspective when appropriate
- Reads like a Wikipedia article or press release

### How to add voice:
- **Have opinions.** React to facts, don't just report them.
- **Vary your rhythm.** Short sentences. Then longer ones. Mix it up.
- **Acknowledge complexity.** Real humans have mixed feelings.
- **Use "I" when it fits.** First person is honest, not unprofessional.
- **Be specific about feelings.** Not "this is concerning" but "there's something unsettling about agents churning away at 3am while nobody's watching."

---

## How to apply

1. Before writing any deliverable or prototype text, read this file
2. After writing, scan your output against the patterns above
3. Fix any matches
4. Read the output aloud – if it sounds like a press release or a LinkedIn post, rewrite it
