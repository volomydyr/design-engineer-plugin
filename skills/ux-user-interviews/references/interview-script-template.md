# Interview Script Template

This template provides a framework for building user interview and survey scripts, built on the three core empathy questions from the Behavior Mapping methodology and the "Replacing X" technique.

---

## Part 1: Three Core Empathy Questions

These three questions are the backbone of any interview. Each one targets a specific dimension of user motivation and ability. They turn structured interviews into natural storytelling, which reveals emotions that closed-ended questions never capture.

Adapt the bracketed parts to match your specific product and problem.

### Question 1: Hope (Motivation)

> "If you had a magic wand and could instantly [get the value your product promises], how would that change your life?"

Reveals why users care – their intrinsic motivation and the emotional outcome they are seeking.

**Example for a learning app:** "If you had a magic wand and could instantly organize all your learning materials – articles, videos, courses – in one place, how would that change your life?"

### Question 2: Pain (Challenge)

> "What is your biggest challenge when it comes to [the problem your product addresses]? And why is it so challenging?"

Reveals the real obstacles users face. Often different from what you assume.

**Example for a learning app:** "What is the hardest part of figuring out what to learn next in your career? Why is it such a challenge?"

### Question 3: Barrier (Past Behavior)

> "Tell me about the last time you tried to [do the action your product enables]. What was it like? What was preventing you from [reaching the goal]?"

Reveals real friction points – the gap between intention and action.

**Example for a learning app:** "Tell me about the last time you tried to stick to a professional development plan. How did it go? What stopped you from finishing it?"

---

## Part 2: The "Replacing X" Technique

Every product replaces something. Even if there is no direct competitor, users were doing something before your product existed. Understanding what you replace reveals the real competitive landscape and the switching costs users face.

Ask: "Before [your product/solution category], how did you handle [the problem]? What tools, habits, or workarounds did you use?"

Users do not compare your product to competitors in a vacuum. They compare it to their current state – which might be a spreadsheet, a sticky note, a conversation with a friend, or simply ignoring the problem.

---

## Part 3: Survey Guidance

When building surveys instead of interviews, adapt the questions above with these rules:

**Use closed questions.** People prefer clicking to typing, so more will finish the form. Keep open-ended ones to a minimum.

**Use Google Forms.** It's simple and exports neatly into CSV, which is perfect for AI-powered analysis later.

**Aim for ~100 respondents.** That's enough for reliable data with a low margin of error. Reach out personally in the right communities, targeting your user persona specifically.

### The Past-Not-Future Rule

Never ask about hypothetical behavior. Always ask about real past experience.

**Wrong:** "Would you pay for an app that could organize your learning materials?"

**Right (sequence of three questions):**
1. "Have you used an app or tool that helps organize learning materials?"
2. "If yes, did you pay for it?"
3. "Was the price worth it? Why or why not?"

The first approach gets you wishes. The second gets you evidence.

**Always collect contacts at the end** – "Would you be willing to participate in a short usability test? Leave your email if yes." This part is easy to forget but critical for the next round of research.
