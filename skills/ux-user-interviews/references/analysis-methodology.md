# Research Analysis Methodology

The dual-analysis approach for interpreting user research results. The core principle: analyze results yourself first, then let AI analyze independently, then combine both.

---

## Why Dual Analysis

Don't just share the testing results with AI and ask for analysis — you'll miss important details and AI will definitely make up things that aren't there.

If you share your conclusions first, AI will confirm them — even when the data does not support them. Your human analysis catches context and nuance that AI misses. AI analysis catches volume-based patterns that humans overlook. Combined, they produce the most reliable insights.

---

## Step 1: Analyze Results Yourself First

Watch every user test recording, read every survey response. Take your own notes and form your own conclusions. Write down:

- Your top 3-5 key findings (in order of importance)
- Supporting evidence for each (specific responses, behaviors, quotes)
- Surprises — things you did not expect
- Concerns — findings that challenge your assumptions

Do NOT share this with AI yet.

---

## Step 2: AI Analyzes Independently

Share the raw data with AI, but NOT your conclusions. Ask AI to:

1. Organize the data into clean tables and charts
2. Spot patterns in behavior and answers
3. Compare those patterns against your earlier assumptions and hypotheses
4. Highlight which ones were confirmed (and which weren't)
5. Summarize key insights with next-step recommendations

This way AI isn't biased by your interpretation.

### Verify AI's Claims

When AI makes a claim, ask it to provide the specific quotes or data points that support it. Then search for those exact data points in your raw results. If you cannot find them, AI probably invented them.

AI will confidently state things like "research proves users prefer quarterly payments" when the data only shows "users dislike monthly subscriptions" — which is not the same claim at all.

---

## Step 3: Compare and Combine

Upload your analysis and ask AI to identify what might have been missed:

> "Here is my independent analysis of the same data. Compare it with your analysis. What did I catch that you missed? What did you catch that I missed? Where do we disagree?"

When your analysis and AI's analysis conflict, go back to the raw data. The data resolves the disagreement, not either analysis. Combine the best results from both versions.

---

## Keep It Simple

Why make it complicated if you can keep it simple? The 80/20 rule works great for MVPs, and it applies to research analysis too.

Don't take AI's word as gospel. Always do a quick manual pass yourself, then compare. The mix of your judgment + AI efficiency is where the real value is.
